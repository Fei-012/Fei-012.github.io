Project Overview / 2026 Project

From the first project to the next steps.

The first version of the project in summer 2025 created real short-term impact: students participated actively, built connections, and became more emotionally open. At the same time, it showed that meaningful change needs continuity, structure, and deeper integration with the community.

Why the Next Phase Is Needed

The 2025 programme was warmly received, but it also revealed its limits. Funding restricted the number of students who could join, the short duration made it hard to sustain emotional and behavioural change, and the project remained only lightly connected to local systems and organisations.

  • 2026 focuses on continuity rather than a one-off intervention.
  • The aim is to build a model that can deepen impact and remain sustainable over time.

Expanding Activities

Students consistently expressed interest in opportunities they rarely had access to, including sports, art, music, reading, creative writing, and other forms of self-expression.

In 2026, these extracurricular elements will not be treated as optional add-ons. They will be part of a broader holistic model designed to improve engagement, support emotional well-being, and build confidence through diverse creative and social experiences.

A Structured Well-being Curriculum

Rather than repeating a short intervention, the next phase will develop a clear and adaptable well-being curriculum. This will provide consistent content for future teaching, make the programme easier to repeat and scale, and support impact beyond individual sessions.

The goal is to move from activity-based intervention to a sustainable educational model with stronger long-term value.

Media, Voice, and Wider Impact

With students' consent, the 2026 phase will also use documentaries, short videos, and visual storytelling to extend the project beyond the classroom. This can help raise awareness of migrant students' experiences, attract support, and give students a stronger platform for sharing their own voices.

In some cases, student-made work may also be displayed or shared more publicly, creating opportunities for creative ownership and additional support for the project.

The Next Phase

The next stage expands the focus from short-term well-being toward long-term educational quality. Aligned with the idea of quality education, it combines well-being work with extracurricular and creative learning opportunities so that emotional support becomes part of a fuller educational environment.

  • Physical activities, music, art, creative writing, and literature can become part of the programme structure.
  • Emotional and social development remain central, but are supported by more continuity and stronger community connection.
