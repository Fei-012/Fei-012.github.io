Project Overview / 2025 Project Report

Growing Up Between Worlds.

In a small coastal town in China, a group of children gathered for what they thought would be another ordinary summer class. By the end of three days, many were sharing stories they had never told before, writing letters to family for the first time, and realising they were not alone.

The System Behind the Story

The project began with a simple question: what happens when children do not have a space to be heard? One reason this question matters is the hukou system, which continues to shape access to education and public services in China by separating rural and urban registration.

For migrant families who move to cities in search of work, this creates a deep contradiction. Parents live and work in urban areas, but their children often cannot access the same schools and support systems as local students. Many instead attend migrant schools that are underfunded, understaffed, and asked to carry far more than their resources allow.

Why Songmen

This first project took place in Songmen, a rapidly growing town in Wenling. It is not one of China's biggest cities, and that is exactly why it matters: it reflects the everyday reality of migrant families living outside the best-resourced urban centres.

As a third-tier industrial city, Wenling attracts large numbers of migrant workers who come for opportunity but still face unequal access to educational support. The growth of migrant families in Songmen made it a clear place to begin this work.

The Invisible Struggle

Before designing the project, conversations with students, parents, and migrant workers pointed to the same pressure: families were working long hours, communities were constantly shifting, and schools were struggling to keep up.

The educational challenge was not only academic. It was emotional, social, and often hidden. Children were growing up between places, expectations, and identities with too few spaces where they could speak honestly about that experience.

What We Did

The three-day programme was designed to move from trust-building to expression and finally toward emotional connection and future imagination.

  • Day 1 focused on belonging through expression trees, identity activities, and storytelling games.
  • Day 2 focused on finding a voice through communication games, emotional awareness exercises, and life-journey mapping.
  • Day 3 focused on love and the future through film discussion, letter writing to family, future-aspiration activities, and a collective celebration.

What Changed

The transformation was not dramatic, but it was real. Students who were quiet, hesitant, or resistant at the beginning became more willing to participate, speak, and try again. One student moved from saying the programme would be boring to saying they felt happy by the end of the first day.

Participation grew, voices became louder, and emotional openness became visible. More than half of the students wrote letters expressing gratitude, many to parents or grandparents they rarely spoke to openly. Most importantly, the project created a safe space in which students could begin to talk about their actual lives.
